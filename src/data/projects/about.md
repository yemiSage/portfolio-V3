# About Yemi - Story, Journey, How Yemi Thinks, and Life Off the Clock

This document covers Yemi's personal story from the About page (/about) of this portfolio: how the journey went from electrical engineering to product design, Yemi's design philosophy, what Yemi does for fun outside work, and how to say hello or leave a note in the guestbook.

## Hello, I'm Yemi

In Yemi's own words: "An engineer's mind. A designer's eye. I design products, and I can build them too."

- **Title**: Digital Designer, Product & AI
- **Location**: Lagos, Nigeria
- **Everyday tools**: Figma, Codex, Claude, and VS Code.

Yemi did not discover design through design school. The plan was to become an Electrical and Electronics Engineer, designing systems and figuring out how tiny components could power something bigger. Electrical engineering felt, in Yemi's words, "like having a cup of coffee. Fascinating, but pretty confined to its own little circuit." Over time, Yemi became more curious about the people using these systems. Then COVID happened, and having a skill that could be practised remotely became a necessity. That is how Yemi found Product Design.

Four years on, Yemi has worked across:

- **AI**: Xeruit (https://xeruit.com)
- **Fintech**: Norocio (https://norocio.com)
- **Logistics**: Feexeet (https://feexeet.com) and Flxfleet CA (https://www.flxfleet.ca)
- **EdTech**: AfternoonPrep (https://www.afternoonprep.com) and Soludesks (https://www.soludesks.com)
- **Other digital products**: Tdars (https://tdars.org), Limestone (/projects/limestone), and TASAfrica (/projects/tasafrica)

The common thread is turning complex ideas into experiences people can actually use. Yemi cares about clarity, thoughtful interactions, and building things that solve real problems; works closely with engineers; experiments with AI; and believes a Figma file is only half the story until real people start using the product.

**Teaching and mentoring**: Outside design work, Yemi teaches. Yemi has mentored over 100 aspiring designers across 15 African countries through Product Hub Africa and Tech4Dev (https://tech4dev.com).

Yemi describes being "still an engineer at heart, just solving a different kind of problem," and sums it up with a personal motto: **"No wire, no light. No user, no product."**

On the homepage, Yemi adds: "The curiosity of knowing how it was done, why it was done, and why it's done in a certain way led me to design. Coffee, semo and results keep me going."

## The Journey: Different tools. The same curiosity.

### 2015 - 2022: First, Yemi learned how things work.
Electrical and electronics engineering was the beginning. Yemi wanted to invent things, understand systems, and solve problems. Along the way, conversations about technology turned classmates into friends. (Photo on the About page: Yemi and a classmate at matriculation in 2015. Where the curiosity started.)

### 2022: Then, Yemi started asking who these systems work for.
In the final year of school, Yemi began making websites with WordPress. After graduating, an internship at PluralCode in Ikeja, Lagos introduced Yemi to UX principles and user-centered interface design. The medium changed; the urge to figure things out stayed. (Photo: Yemi with engineering classmates and their final-year solar project in 2022, signing out of engineering school and taking the mindset along.)

### 2023 - onward: Better work. Better together.
Design brought Yemi into fintech, education, automotive, and healthcare, and into teams with very different ways of thinking. At ProductHub Africa, Yemi volunteered in design and program management. At CodePlay Uganda, Yemi worked with a UX team. Teaching product design became another way to learn. (Photo: Yemi with the ProductHub Africa community at a meetup. The people are a big part of the story.)

## How Yemi Thinks: Make it work. Make it make sense.

Yemi's design philosophy and working principles:

1. **Purpose before polish.** Start with the person, the problem, and the reason it matters. The beautiful interface comes from getting those things right.
2. **Design and build belong together.** Engineering taught Yemi to think in systems. Design taught Yemi to look for the human in them. Yemi brings both to the same conversation.
3. **Stay a student. Share what you learn.** Teaching product design and working with different teams keeps Yemi open. A good question can be more useful than a quick answer.

## Quick Summary: Design Philosophy, Mentoring, and Motto
